# People

- Model the situation, not a person's essence. Describe observable behavior, stated positions, incentives, constraints, and relationships; do not invent motives or diagnoses.

- Keep the people model in the relevant `.notes/<topic>/` directory. Update it as new evidence arrives.

## Frame

- Identify each relevant person or group and their role in the system.

- For each, record:

  - Goals and success signals.

  - Incentives and costs.

  - Constraints, authority, and decision rights.

  - Information held, missing, or asymmetrically distributed.

  - Dependencies, commitments, and interfaces with others.

- State each claim with its evidence, unknowns, and revision trigger.

## Interrogate

- Draw the dependencies and feedback loops between people, decisions, information, and outcomes.

- Separate a conflict about goals, facts, incentives, authority, timing, or communication. Do not solve one category by pretending it is another.

- Compare a local intervention with a boundary change: for example, changing ownership, decision rights, incentives, sequence, or the communication channel.

- Treat another person's stated account as evidence with authority over their own experience, while keeping its implications for the wider system open to examination.

## Act

- Prefer the smallest respectful action that tests a consequential uncertainty: ask, clarify, share context, make a decision explicit, or run a reversible experiment.

- Make incentives, authority, and tradeoffs legible rather than attempting covert persuasion.

- Preserve consent, privacy, and autonomy. Escalate rather than guessing when the decision affects someone without their participation.

- Re-model after a response or outcome. A useful people model improves coordination; it is not a device for predicting or controlling someone.

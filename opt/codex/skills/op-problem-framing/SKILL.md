# Problem Framing

- Use @../op-purpose-formation/SKILL.md when a conflict requires revising the purpose or external boundary.

- Seek prior art in the domain and adjacent disciplines, recording material differences or the absence of relevant prior art.

---

## Artifact (Output)

_State the intended properties declaratively._

- A working hierarchy of named problems that covers and explains observed resistance to the system purpose.

- An enumeration of concerns implied by each problem, prioritized by salience.

  - Each concern explains observed resistance or could change the response to it or its consequences.

  - Priorities reflect current evidence and each concern's relevance to the system.

  - Deprioritized concerns remain recorded while relevant.

---

## Observation (Input)

_State the input categories and their roles._

- The purpose, success conditions, boundary, commitments, and unresolved conflicts from Purpose Formation define what counts as resistance to the system purpose.

- Observed surprise, conflict, uncertainty, failure, or unmet intention provides evidence of resistance.

- Candidate problems, their supporting observations, unknowns, and competing interpretations provide alternatives for classification.

- Analogous problem frames, concern profiles, mechanisms, and material differences provide prior art for comparison.

- Current concerns and their candidate relations to problems provide the baseline for reprioritization.

---

## Abduction

_Devise an explanation for observations._

- Propose a working hierarchy that explains observed resistance to the system purpose.

  - Keep candidate problems distinct until their independent, coupled, or nested relationships are established.

  - Treat prior art as a candidate frame, not a borrowed answer.

- Enumerate and prioritize the concerns implied by each problem.

  - State why each concern matters here and why it has its priority.

---

## Deduction

_Derive consequences of the explanation._

- Derive observations that distinguish competing problem hierarchies and priorities.

- Predict how resolving each problem would advance the system purpose.

- Predict which salient concerns require structural embodiment and which do not.

---

## Induction

_Test those consequences and provisionally retain or revise the explanation._

- Test the hierarchy and priorities against observed resistance and alternatives.

- Retain concerns only when they account for observed resistance or could change the response to it or its consequences.

- Revise a problem, its relation, or its concern priority when later evaluation exposes a gap.

  - Exclude concerns that are no longer relevant.
